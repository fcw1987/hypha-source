Choose an activity

Open New Thread and choose what you want to play. Each activity keeps its own thread, rules and membership. You can use the same thread for another visit.

Quick play
Start a one-court singles match or a small doubles night. Four friends can rotate partners across three games and save each result at courtside.
Round robin
Choose scheduled opponents in singles or fixed doubles, or use a rotating-partner mixer. A published court sheet lets people find their wave before earlier results arrive.
Ladder
Use each round’s winners to move people between courts, or complete a block of games within a flight before movement. Preview the next assignments, then publish them.
Tournament
Start with a seeded elimination bracket or use pool games to qualify entrants for playoffs. Review the actual bracket before announcing the next games.
Practice log
Record minutes or successful repetitions for a drill. You can start solo and keep practice separate from competitions.

Four friends, one court

For an evening with the same friends, choose Quick play in New Thread. Pick singles for a head-to-head game or doubles to switch partners after each game.

  1. Start a doubles night. Unlock Hypha, open New Thread → Quick play and choose doubles. Name the group, set the date and enter four names. Leave Available courts at one. For a short night, open Thread rules and choose three rounds.
  2. Check the three matchups. In a full four-player cycle, each person partners each other person once and faces each of them twice. More rounds repeat this cycle with a fresh opportunity to score.
  3. Save the first result. Suppose Fern and Moss beat Wren and Lark 11–7 in their assigned game. Enter 11 for Fern and Moss, 7 for Wren and Lark, then tap Save game. Both winners earn 11 points and both opponents earn 7. One final counts as one game played for each person.
  4. Keep the thread for next week. Score the remaining games, then submit the session. Open Recap for tonight’s totals or Next session when you play again. Choose Stats to explore partnerships once you have several results.

Open your workspace

Open the official Hypha app and enter the shared site passphrase. The app works in a browser without ChatGPT sign-in. The passphrase opens your workspace. Group threads each have their own membership.

Starting or joining a group

  • Start a group. Open the thread sidebar, or the thread drawer on a phone, and choose New Thread. A new thread is private until its creator issues an invite.
  • Join a group. Open the full private invite link from its creator. Unlock Hypha if prompted, check which role the invite offers, and enter the four-digit thread passcode if requested. You can also paste the full link into Join a thread. Once admitted, reopen it from your sidebar.
  • Keep your place. A browser workspace uses that browser profile and its cookie. Use the same profile to return. A new device, private browsing or cleared cookies opens a separate workspace. If your threads belong to a Sites account, use Resume a ChatGPT workspace, sign in to the same account, then unlock Hypha.

Choose what an invite allows

Owner
Scores and manages the thread, creates or disables invites, removes participants, ends an open season, and moves or restores the thread in Trash.
Scorekeeper
Can start games, post live scores, save or correct finals, manage sessions and view score history.
Read-only viewer
Can follow schedules, results, standings and Stats, and can email a submitted recap. Only owners manage thread invites.

Owners choose Share, select a role, then create that role’s invite. Require a thread passcode starts on. Give the four-digit passcode separately from the link. With that setting off, joining still requires the private link and a valid site session.

What changes when I rotate or disable an invite?

A role’s link is reusable until the owner rotates or disables it. Changing the passcode requirement disables outstanding invites for both roles. Create fresh links for future participants. Already admitted members keep their role, even if they open a different role’s link. Remove all participants and invites withdraws member access and disables the links. The owner retains the thread.

Start at the Hypha home page in your usual browser. You can open Home Screen setup below the password field or in the thread drawer for device instructions.

Android

If the old artwork stays on your iPhone, keep that icon. Save any pending scores there first. Open the home page in Safari, reload it, then use Add to Home Screen again. Check the new lime pickleball and verify that the workspace you expect opens before deciding what to do with an older icon.

Your workspace and drafts

A Home Screen app can have separate browser storage. Unlock with the site passphrase if prompted. For a browser-owned group, return to the original browser or use the group’s invite. An invite grants its specified role. For account-owned groups, resume the same ChatGPT workspace. Unsaved score drafts belong to the browser or Home Screen app where you entered them.

Playing with a weak connection

An already open scorecard can hold edits on your device when local storage is available. A draft labeled not synced has not reached your group. After reconnecting, review the current result and save it yourself. A fresh offline launch after a successful online visit opens connection help. Set up quick play

In New Thread → Quick play, name the thread, choose the date and enter distinct player labels. Use a distinguishing initial for two people with the same first name. In doubles, setup starts with four labeled fields. Add Players adds up to two at a time. Extra rows can be removed individually or left blank.

Singles

2–16 players, one court. With two, the first matchup is ready. With more, choose First player and Opponent. Save the game, then Add rematch or select the next two and Add game. Submit after one game for a one-off, or continue the thread on another date.

Rotating partners

4–16 players. Hypha generates doubles matchups, balances appearances and varies partners and opponents. A four-player rotation cycles through all three partnerships. A complete five-player cycle seats each player once. A longer or shorter session uses the rounds selected in Thread rules.

Fixed teams

Assign everyone to a named pair. Each pair plays the other pairs once. Four teams on two courts play three rounds. An odd team count produces a team bye. With fewer courts, matches run in waves. Team session standings stay separate from the individual season race.

Available courts starts at one. Doubles can use two with eight attendees, three with twelve and four with sixteen. Pick the number actually available. A player scheduled on another active court must finish there before starting a conflicting matchup.

Same group, another night

After submitting, choose Next session. For doubles, check who is present, add one-night guests and confirm the courts. To handle a late arrival or departure, use Change attendance → Preview changes. Review the matchups before applying. Started and scored games stay in place. Fewer than four eligible players pauses future doubles play. Fixed-team attendance changes use whole pairs. For singles, confirm the attending names and choose the opening matchup for the new night.

Game to 11, win by two, with no season target. The owner may end an open season from the latest submitted Session results using End season and its confirmation.

For a points target, Hypha checks completed rounds in schedule order. Courts in the same round are simultaneous. By default, players crossing together share the finish. Earlier threads retain their configured tie rule. Unplayed games after a decided finish contribute no results.

Round robin and ladder formats

To arrange a schedule up front, choose New Thread → Round robin. To use completed results for movement, choose New Thread → Ladder. Both activities support singles, fixed teams and rotating doubles with the options below. Each new event thread has an open season. A dated session can finish while its season continues.

Rotating partners

Partner mixer issues the schedule up front. Choose 1–24 waves. Hypha balances appearances and varies partners and opponents within the available courts and time. Short schedules and odd attendance can leave repeat matchups or unequal appearances.

Court ladder uses the last results to decide where people play next. The draw prioritizes splitting the previous partnerships. Preview the actual new partners on each court.

Fixed teams

Use Choose fixed teams to name pairs and check who plays together. Round robin puts each pair against the other pairs in its pool over a full draw. Court ladder moves the pair together. Check in whole pairs for both formats.

Singles

Each court holds two players. Choose Round robin for scheduled opponents or Court ladder for a new court based on the result. Singles records each person’s own score and opponent. For a quick manual matchup or a rematch on one court, use Quick play → Singles.

Full, shortened or pooled round robin

In Round robin settings, choose up to four Pools, with at least two entrants in each. An entrant is a singles player or a fixed team. Hypha deals the registration list across the pools in order and keeps sizes within one entrant of each other. A full draw plays every pair of entrants within its pool once. An odd pool gives someone a bye each pairing round. Limited rounds takes the requested number of pairing rounds per pool. When pool places will determine a playoff bracket, choose New Thread → Tournament → Qualify through pools before starting.

Score-based or timed games

In Event scoring, Score-based uses the points-to-win and finish rule. For Timed, choose the minutes per wave. Optionally choose Finish early at a point cap. A cap of 11 allows an 11–10 finish before the whistle. Without a cap the sides can keep scoring throughout the timed period.

On the current unfinished round or wave, choose Start …-minute clock when play begins. The board shows a countdown and the whistle time. Returning to the board uses the saved start time. An external whistle also works. At time, record the actual unequal score, such as 7–5. At 6–6, play one deciding point and enter 7–6. Expiry alone records no score and moves nobody.

Box ladders and flight standings

Choose singles or fixed teams for a Box ladder. Enter the opening seed order from strongest flight to weakest. Choose two to four flights with at least three players or three intact pairs in each flight. Each entrant meets the others in that flight once per block. The field stays fixed across blocks within this session. When courts are scarce, one block spans several waves.

Open Flight standings to see the record and average point margin per game. Hypha ranks by win percentage, then by points scored minus points allowed per game. Equal values on both measures share the displayed rank. For movement only, the earlier original seed receives the higher slot in a remaining tie.

After every game in the block has a saved final, preview the next block. The leading entrant in each lower flight moves up one flight. The last entrant in the flight above moves down. At the top and bottom nobody can move beyond the field. Review Proposed flights and publish the block, or finish the session at the completed boundary. A later correction updates records while the issued next block keeps its entrants.

Box rules vary by organizer. RIPL uses eight-person groups and moves its top two and bottom two between later events. Hypha’s block rule exchanges one entrant across each adjacent boundary. RIPL’s published box format

Read a player’s event recap

Open Recap → Player recap and choose a name. Wins–losses and point margin use that person’s saved finals. For example, winning 11–8 on Court 2 earns one win and a +3 margin. If the next draw puts that player on Court 1, the assignment changes immediately. Their highest court played stays at Court 2 until a Court 1 final is saved. A checked-in player who sits through a completed round earns a wait and keeps the same W–L.

On a court ladder, Recent courts traces up to eight saved appearances. In a round robin, the recap shows how many scheduled opponents the player has faced. The results list gives the exact scores and opponents in pages of eight. Corrections recalculate these figures from the saved games.

Who moves on a ladder?

Win up / Lose down
Winners move up one active court and losers down one. Court 1 is the top. Top winners hold their court. Bottom losers stay at the bottom when nobody is waiting.
Win up / Top court falls
Top winners stay. Other winners move up. Top-court losers go to the bottom and other losers hold their court when there is space. This changes the cost of losing on Court 1.
One playing court
All moves lead back to the same court. Rotating doubles can switch partners. Fixed teams or two singles players can have a rematch. Waiting entrants can take an available place.
A waiting list
Hypha fills whole games. Opening extras wait in registration order. At the next draw, existing waiters have priority over new arrivals. With a full set of courts, the losing side destined for the bottom yields places first. Fixed pairs enter or wait together. A changing headcount can shift other lower-court assignments. Use the published board to direct players.
Why do clubs describe “Humpty Dumpty” differently?

At East County in Oregon the name describes timed rounds with winners moving up, losers moving down and partners splitting. Good Game Sports uses the name for top-court losers falling to the bottom while other losing sides stay. Jumbo’s Portland describes that top-court fall with partners switching and waiting players entering at the lowest court. Announce the movement rule and the waiting rule at check-in. From pool play to the final

Choose New Thread → Tournament, then singles or fixed teams. The draw holds 2–48 singles players or 2–24 fixed pairs. Qualify through pools gives everyone matches before elimination. Start with seeds goes straight to a knockout bracket. Review the entry order and physical courts at setup. For fixed teams, an optional open or mixed division label describes the pairs you enter.

Two pools of four teams

For 16 players, choose fixed teams and make eight named pairs. Select two qualifying pools. Hypha deals the entry order across Pool A and Pool B. Each team meets the other three in its pool once. Four teams make six games per pool, or 12 pool games in total.

  1. Finish pool play. With all eight teams active, save all 12 finals and check Pool standings. The top two teams in each pool qualify. A single-pool tournament can instead advance the top two or, with enough entrants, the top four.
  2. Resolve qualifying ties. Within each pool, rank by wins, then wins against the other entrants tied on wins. Next compare total points scored minus points allowed across the pool, then that same difference in games among the original tied group. If all four measures leave a tie affecting qualification or seed order, use Resolve playoff seeding. Confirm the order within that tied group and enter a reason before previewing. Places entirely below qualification can remain tied.
  3. Check the crossed semifinals. Pool A’s winner meets Pool B’s runner-up. Pool B’s winner meets Pool A’s runner-up. For example, if Maples and Alders finish first and second in A, and Firs and Oaks do so in B, the games are Maples vs Oaks and Firs vs Alders. Preview the semifinals, then publish the draw.
  4. Play the medal games. After the semifinal finals are saved, preview and publish the championship game between the winners. With the bronze option enabled, the losing semifinalists play for third. The full example has 15 played games without bronze or 16 with it. Save the final scores, then choose Submit tournament results.

A player or team leaves pool play

Before the first playoff stage is published, an owner or scorekeeper can open Withdraw or restore a player on the tournament board. For fixed doubles the control reads Withdraw or restore a team and applies to the whole pair. Choose the entrant under Pool player or Pool team, tap Preview withdrawal, check the affected games and active pool size, then choose Confirm withdrawal.

A played final stays in the session record and player statistics, and its score can still be corrected. For playoff qualification Hypha excludes that game for both sides, including wins and point differences used to break ties. In the eight-pair draw above, suppose Oaks leave after playing Firs and every game between the remaining teams is final. Ten of the twelve scheduled games were played. Nine count toward qualification. The Firs–Oaks final remains in both teams’ records, and Oaks’ two unfinished games become void. Nobody receives a forfeit win or points for either void game. Open Games affected by withdrawals to see each final or void game. Owners and scorekeepers can also see the entrant and pool in History → Score history.

To bring an entrant back, select them, tap Preview restore, then Confirm restore. Earlier finals count toward qualification again and unfinished games return to their original assignments, including saved in-progress scores. A game involving another withdrawn entrant stays excluded or void. If restoring a started game would double-book a player or court, finish the overlapping match and preview again.

Each pool must still supply its own qualifiers. If Pool A needs two teams but has only one active team, Hypha names the shortage and holds the playoffs until enough teams are restored. There is no wildcard from another pool. Publishing the first playoff stage closes withdrawal and restoration and freezes the seed order.

Direct seeded singles and byes

For six singles players, choose Start with seeds. Open Seed order, move entrants into order, and inspect Opening matchups. Six entrants fill an eight-slot bracket with two opening byes. The top two seeds advance automatically and four players contest the other two slots. A bye needs no score and adds no game, point, win or loss. Preview and publish each stage once its entrants are known.

What can a score correction change?

Before publishing a playoff stage, correct a final and preview again. Publishing the opening knockout records the seeds and any qualifying tie decisions. A later pool correction changes the visible pool results while those published seeds and assignments stay fixed. After the next knockout stage is published, a correction to a game feeding it must retain its winner. The points can still be corrected under the selected finish rule. Owners and scorekeepers can use the usual correction flow on submitted results.

Hypha uses the recreational four-step pool rule and recorded tie decision described above. For a sanctioned event, check the official eligibility, withdrawal, ranking and match-format requirements. Rule 15.B.4.a covers withdrawals, retirements and forfeits in sanctioned round robins. Hypha keeps local played-game statistics. USA Pickleball’s 2026 rules also distinguish round robin, pool play leading to a playoff, and a qualifying round robin without separate pools. Run the court board

One iPad can serve as a shared courtside display. Scorekeepers can use their phones to enter results. Keep the thread private or use its existing role-based invites for scorekeepers and read-only spectators.

  1. Check people in before the draw. Enter one name per line. For a ladder, order names from the top court downward. Use a last initial for duplicate first names. In Check-in list, uncheck absentees and mark one-night guests. Scheduled singles accepts 2–48 attendees and 1–24 physical courts. Scheduled doubles accepts 4–48 attendees and 1–12 courts. Fixed teams require complete pairs. Only full games occupy a court. Review the active, waiting and unused counts before creating the selected activity.
  2. Find a court or player. Read the round or wave number and the count of courts final. Use Where do I go? with part of a name or a court number. Court cards show the two sides, court status and available scores. The player lookup distinguishes an absent player from a player sitting out. Check the ordered Waiting list on the current ladder round.
  3. Record each actual final. Tap Score Court on the appropriate card. A posted live score can help spectators follow play. Only a saved final affects the standings. If a scheduled court says Waiting, finish the earlier game that needs its players. Back to court board returns to the assignments.
  4. Review before sending players to a new ladder round. Finish every active court. For a late arrival or departure, open Next round attendance. In rotating doubles and singles, you can check returning players back in or add late guests. In fixed doubles, check registered pairs in or out together and introduce new pairs at the next session. Choose Preview round, check the courts and waiting list, then Publish round. A preview alone does not issue assignments.
  5. Close the event and read it back. After every issued game has a final, choose Finish session → Submit results. For a court ladder or box ladder, finish before publishing another round or block. A scheduled mixer or round robin needs its issued draw completed. For a tournament, finish the medal games and use Submit tournament results. On the submitted results screen check completed scores and the recap. Next session brings the roster back for a new event.
What if someone corrects a result or changes the board?

An owner or scorekeeper can correct an active court or use Correct a score in submitted results. A corrected final changes the standings. If it changes the winner before the next ladder round is published, preview again to see the new assignments. Every published assignment stays in place, including a round that has not started. A published round-robin, mixer or flight block keeps its issued roster and matchups. If attendance changes prevent a scheduled match, leave it pending.

If another scorer saves during a preview or confirmation, refresh the workspace and review the latest board before repeating that action. Reopen the same thread after a reload to see the server’s last published round and saved results. An unpublished preview, a device draft and an unsent email each need their own deliberate next step.

32 players, eight courts, two hours

This example is a rotating doubles ladder. All 32 people can play each round. Eight complete rounds would produce 64 finals and eight games per player, assuming the same attendance throughout.

A 6–8 p.m. booking

  1. 6:00–6:06
    Check in and brief the courts.

    Open New Thread → Ladder and paste 32 names. Choose Rotating partners → Court ladder, eight physical courts and Win up / Lose down. In Event scoring, choose Timed, 12 minutes per wave, with no point cap. Name Court 1 as the top. Show people where to find their court and partner.

  2. 6:06–6:18
    Play the opening round.

    Start the 12-minute clock on the board. At the whistle, record the score on each court. If the sides are tied, play one deciding point before saving the final. Allow two minutes for scores and court changes.

  3. 6:18–6:20
    Save, preview and move.

    Once all eight courts have a final, preview Round 2. With full attendance, Court 4’s next group contains the winners from Court 5 and the losers from Court 3. Each new pair can combine one player from each arriving side. Check the published names and send players to their courts.

  4. 6:20–7:56
    Repeat while time permits.

    Following the same budget, the last round starts at 7:44. Check the waiting list if the headcount changes. When another round is unlikely to fit, finish after the last completed round.

  5. 7:56–8:00
    Submit and review.

    Choose Finish session, check the completed-game count, then Submit results. Open the recap and compare appearances before comparing points.

Timing assumption. Eight 12-minute games per court plus seven two-minute changes use 110 minutes, leaving ten minutes for check-in and a closing buffer. The timer helps pace the round. Each court still needs a saved final. A deciding point or slow change can use the buffer. Run fewer rounds if needed. East County publishes a 12-minute play and two-minute change pattern under its own timed scoring rules. East County program format

Record a result courtside

  1. Open the court. Scorecard → Courtside shows who is playing, who is up next and who is sitting. Start game marks a game on court. A waiting court identifies the one its players are finishing on.
  2. Enter both scores. Tap a common-score chip below each side, use plus and minus, or type a whole number. A chip changes just that side’s draft. Type or step beyond the target when the finish rule allows it.
  3. Choose live or final. Post live score lets admitted members follow play. Save game records the final, updates points and advances the court when players are free. A live score adds nothing to standings until the final is saved.
  4. Close the night. Full sheet accepts scores in batches. Save entered scores saves edited finals and leaves the session open. Submit session saves the eligible batch and opens the results. Complete pending games before submitting unless the race already ended.

Fix a score

In an active session, select the saved game and save its correction. A submitted session opens as a read-only results sheet. Owners and scorekeepers choose Correct a score, select one game and save or cancel. Saving preserves submission and recalculates standings, the finish and Stats. If another scorer changed that game, review the current value before trying again.

Undo last result is available to the scorer who made the most recent eligible save for up to ten minutes, provided no newer thread change has intervened. It restores the prior score or game state. History → Score history shows recorded saves, corrections, undo and submission with thread-local role labels and local times. Earlier scores can predate the timeline.

Find the answer behind a score

Who leads the season?
Standings ranks regular players by cumulative points in singles or rotating doubles. Equal point totals share a rank. Each singles player earns their own final score. Named teams have their own fixed-session standings. A bye adds no points or appearance.
What happened tonight?
Recap shows points this session, games played, points per game and applicable season progress. The denominator includes only saved games the player or team played. Session guests have their own row for that night. Table gives the exact numbers.
When did the lead move?
Progress → This session plots cumulative earned points by game. Season shows each regular player’s gap from that night’s leader. Zero is the lead. Tap player cards to change visible lines, choose a checkpoint, or use the exact checkpoint table. Pending and unplayed games keep their labels.
Which partners play best together?
Stats opens with the highest partnership win rate and each player’s record apart. The default ranking starts at three games together. Equal values share rank and smaller samples are available separately. Rank by can instead show average margin, total wins or frequency. Average margin is points for minus points against, divided by games played.
Did the opposition differ?
Tap a pair for Together vs apart and Who they faced. Tap a player for partner and opponent W–L and recent form. In With each partner → Compare two partners, select two partners. Each shared-opponent row holds the opposing pair constant within the chosen thread, period and format. It shows W–L, margin and count. Sparse results and differing schedules limit the comparison.

Use Period for one session or the whole season. Rotating and fixed doubles are analyzed separately. Singles shows opponent head-to-head. Stats uses eligible saved finals, including finals in an active session. Corrections replace their original results. Guests keep their session identities. Use a comparable game rule when comparing timed and score-based sessions.

Reading results for your role

As a player
Compare wins, games played and average point margin for a pairing over one night and the whole season. A 2–0 pairing has two observations. Check the opponents and look for repeat meetings before drawing a conclusion.
As a director
Read appearances alongside points when someone waited or missed a round. Use the board’s court and attendance details to understand where players were assigned. A player can climb the ladder with wins while the season points table follows the points earned.
As a coach or competitive analyst
Compare repeated opponents within the same scoring rules. Game finals can describe consistency of outcomes, margins and frequency of play. Rally sequence, serve or return quality, shot location, individual contribution and official rating require additional observations. A partnership record alone cannot establish chemistry or the reason for a win.

Log a practice set

Open New Thread → Practice log to start a solo or shared log. In an existing scoring thread, open Practice. Practice entries are visible to that thread’s admitted members. Owners and scorekeepers can record or edit them. Training entries leave match scores, season standings and partnership records unchanged.

  1. Record what you observed. Choose Log practice, name the drill, choose the player or group, and set the date. For a target-serve drill, enter the successful reps and total attempts. You can also record minutes, or choose Minutes only for a drill without a count.
  2. Keep the measure consistent. Use Goal and note for the target or conditions you want to remember. For example, “serve into the deep half” defines a different task from landing anywhere in the service court.
  3. Compare the same drill. In Practice recap, filter by one drill and the same player or group to see measured results across dates. If two sets that day were 6 of 10 and 40 of 50, the day’s result is 46 of 60. A minutes-only entry contributes time and has no success rate.

Use observed counts to choose a drill to revisit. A change in practice percentage does not isolate what improved in a match. For a two-person exercise using a half-court target, see PlayPickleball’s skinny-singles drill.

Send someone the results

Open a submitted session from History or Scorecard, then choose Share results by email. Review the plain-text standings and completed game scores. Add one recipient if you like. Open email draft hands the draft to your device’s configured email app, where you choose whether to send it.

Copy recap copies the entire recap. For a lengthy sheet, copy it first, choose Open draft for pasting and paste it into the message. If copying is blocked, Hypha selects the text so you can use the device’s Copy command. The optional address clears when you close the dialog. The email includes results and player names. Reading it grants no access to the thread.

Terms at the court

Thread
A private group with its saved sessions, rules and roles. A new thread starts a separate history. Practice logs can also stand alone.
Season
The competition across sessions in one thread. Some seasons finish at a points target. An open season can run until its owner ends it.
Session
One dated night or visit. Submit the session to open its results. The next session can use the same thread.
Event
A dated round robin, ladder or tournament with check-in, physical courts and a board of assignments.
Round
One set of ladder matchups, or one pairing turn across the entrants in a round robin. A round robin with few courts may need several waves for one turn.
Wave
A set of scheduled games that can share the available courts at the same time. Find its assignments by wave number on the board.
Flight and block
A flight is a group of entrants at one level of a box ladder. A block is the full set of games inside the current flights, before promotion and relegation.
Seed and bye
A seed is an entrant’s position in the draw. A bye gives an entrant no game in that round. In an elimination bracket an opening bye advances the entrant to the next stage.
Game
One singles or doubles matchup yielding a final score. A saved live score, a pending game and a bye add no final points.
Court
A physical playing space that can host one game at a time. On a ladder, Court 1 is the top active court.

A standings-driven alternative

For a short gathering, a shortened round robin, court ladder and Swiss draw set future opponents in different ways. Hypha offers the first two.

Swiss or standings-driven draw
Pair entrants with similar records after each round and limit repeated opponents. This can produce a competitive short event without completing every possible pairing. Byes, ties in the standings and avoiding prior opponents need explicit pairing rules. PicklePairings’ format descriptions

When something needs a second look

My saved threads aren’t in the sidebar.

Check the workspace label and browser profile first. A shared passphrase can unlock many independent workspaces. For browser-owned threads, return to the original profile with its cookies. For an account-owned thread, resume that account and unlock again. An invite admits a new browser with the invite’s role. It cannot transfer ownership.

The invite asks for another code, or the link stopped working.

The site passphrase and optional thread passcode are separate. Open the creator’s complete private link and use the passcode supplied for that invite. Ask the creator for a current link if its settings changed. A trashed thread pauses access and invite redemption. Repeated wrong entries can temporarily delay another attempt.

A court is waiting or the app rejects a final.

Look for a player still on another court and finish that game first. Check the points-to-win setting and required lead in Rules. At a hard cap of 11, 11–10 is valid. Under win by two without an extended ceiling, tied scores require more play and a 12–10 final is valid.

I lost the connection while recording a score.

Check whether the game says Draft on this device · not synced or Saved game. Browser storage can retain score drafts for that workspace, thread and session on the same device. Reconnect, review the latest score, and save deliberately. Other players see saved finals and posted live scores. Clearing browser storage can remove drafts and browser identity.

How do I restore a thread?

The owner opens Thread options → Move to Trash to move a thread after confirming its name. It stays in the owner’s Trash. Restore brings back the scores, history, memberships and active invites. While it is in Trash, members cannot open it through their usual links.

How can I use these views by keyboard or on a small screen?

Use Tab to reach named inputs, buttons, links and player cards. Use Enter or Space on buttons and disclosure controls. In Progress, focus the plot and use the left and right arrow keys, or step through the exact details below it. The Recap table and Stats records give numeric alternatives to charts. On a phone, use the thread drawer for group navigation and swipe the thread-section strip if a tab is offscreen.
